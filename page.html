    English Commentary
    September 10, 2026

    Just two days after the nine-year anniversary of the club being founded, Sabah Baku make their UEFA Champions League (UCL) main draw debut at the iconic home of returning three-time champions Manchester United. This will be both sides’ first meeting with opposition from the other’s nation. English teams have never lost against Azerbaijani counterparts in Europe (W10, D1).


    Manchester United are back in the UCL after a two-season absence, becoming the first English side to bring up 300 games in the competition here in what is their 26th appearance, also a record among their compatriots. Crashing out at this equivalent stage on their last appearance in 2023/24, the Red Devils have fallen out of love with the UCL having picked up just one win in their last nine such matches (D3, L5). That’s a record they desperately need to improve on after twice squandering the lead in a 2-2 draw at Everton on Sunday, and a return home should give them the necessary respite as they’ve won nine of ten matches at Old Trafford since Michael Carrick’s return (L1), while they’ve also won six successive European home games, scoring 20 goals in the process.

    Fixtures like these are what dreams are made of for Sabah, who’ll also take on Barcelona later on in what is their first-ever major European main draw appearance. Founded in 2017, the Owls were crowned Azerbaijani champions for the first time last term and have more than earned their place in the league phase after coming through four qualifying rounds already this term. Sabah did lose the away leg of their two most recent ties, and while there are no expectations on their shoulders whatsoever, a storming start to their domestic league season that’s seen them net eight unanswered goals (W3) should have them travelling with quiet confidence.

    Manchester United failed to win the opening game of each of their last four European campaigns (D1, L3). Manchester United’s last seven home matches saw both teams score and over 2.5 goals. Sabah have only failed to score in three of their previous 11 European away games. Four of Sabah’s last five UCL fixtures produced over 10.5 total corners.

    Benjamin Šeško returned to the scoresheet at the weekend with the eighth of his last ten Manchester United goals to come on or beyond the hour mark, though three of his last four UCL strikes were match openers inside the first 30 minutes. Veljko Simić is the only player in the Sabah squad with more than one appearance in the UCL proper, and he scored in six of their eight qualifiers, opening the scoring in five of them.

    Amad Diallo remains out for Manchester United, while Sabah look to have no fresh issues.
